Explore the Research
Home / Objective
Objective

This project investigates the relationship between screen time habits and sleep quality among young adults and raises awareness through data driven insights.

🔬

Research Goal

To understand how screen exposure before sleep impacts sleep duration, quality and overall daytime functioning in students.

📢

Awareness Goal

To present findings in an accessible way that encourages students to reflect on their screen habits and make informed changes.

📊

Data Collection

Survey responses gathered from students will be analysed to identify patterns between screen use and reported sleep outcomes.

💡

Practical Outcomes

Evidence based recommendations and awareness materials to help students improve their sleep through small lifestyle changes.

Home / Scope
Scope

The boundaries and focus areas of this research project.

01

Target Group

Students and young adults who regularly use digital devices as part of their daily routine.

02

Focus Area

Screen usage patterns before and during sleep hours including device type, duration and content consumed.

03

Health Metrics

Self-reported sleep quality, duration, tiredness levels and perceived impact on academic performance.

04

Data Method

Small scale survey research via Google Forms with quantitative analysis carried out in Microsoft Excel.

05

Deliverables

Awareness posters, a project report, presentation slides and this website as the primary outputs.

06

Limitations

Findings are based on self-reported data and represent a limited sample. Results are indicative rather than clinically conclusive.

References

A mix of academic research and trusted health sources used to support the content of this project.

01
World Health Organization
Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour

WHO guidelines on sedentary screen-based behaviour and its links to physical and mental health outcomes in young people.

02
Sleep Foundation
Blue Light and Sleep

An overview of how blue light emitted from screens suppresses melatonin production and delays the body's natural sleep onset.

03
Harvard Medical School
Blue Light Has a Dark Side

Harvard Health Publishing examines the science behind blue light exposure and its measurable effect on circadian rhythms and sleep quality.

04
National Sleep Foundation
Sleep in America Poll

Annual survey data on sleep habits across age groups, with specific findings on the role of technology use in disrupted sleep patterns.

05
PubMed — National Library of Medicine
Evening Use of Light-Emitting eReaders Negatively Affects Sleep

Peer-reviewed study published in PNAS showing that light-emitting device use before bed reduces sleepiness, delays the circadian clock and impairs next-morning alertness.

06
American Academy of Sleep Medicine
Sleep and Teens

Fact sheet on recommended sleep durations for teenagers and the documented consequences of insufficient sleep on cognitive function and academic outcomes.

07
Healthline
The Effects of Sleep Deprivation on Your Body

A comprehensive breakdown of how chronic sleep deprivation affects the immune system, mood, cognitive performance and long-term physical health.

08
University of Chicago — Research
Sleep Loss and Academic Performance

Research findings linking insufficient sleep in college students to lower GPAs, reduced class attendance and impaired memory consolidation after studying.
